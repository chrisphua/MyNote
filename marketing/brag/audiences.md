# Audiences

Three groups, in order of how likely they are to pay. Each wants something
different; copy that tries to address all three at once lands with none of them.

## 1. The offline-irritated

**Who:** commuters, travellers, people on bad rural connections, anyone who has
watched a note app spin while they had a thought to write down.

**What they want:** to type, immediately, always.

**Lead with:** "Works on a plane." Show airplane mode in the first two seconds.

**They buy:** sync — because they own two devices and neither is reliably online.

## 2. The customisers

**Who:** people who have opinions about typefaces, who have rearranged their home
screen more than once, who install themes for everything.

**What they want:** to make it theirs.

**Lead with:** the theme editor. Show a page changing live under a colour drag.

**They buy:** the $9.99 unlock, usually within a day of installing. Highest-intent
group and the cheapest to serve — themes cost nothing to run.

## 3. The cross-platform stranded

**Who:** iPhone plus Android tablet, or a work Android and a personal iPhone.
Apple Notes strands them, and they know it.

**What they want:** one place their notes actually live.

**Lead with:** "Buy once, on either platform."

**They buy:** sync, and they are the least likely to churn because switching costs
them the thing they came for.

---

## Who this is not for

Say so plainly rather than over-promising to them:

- Teams wanting shared, collaborative editing. Not built.
- People who need a web client. iOS and Android only.
- Anyone wanting AI writing help. Not built, not planned.
- Databases, kanban boards and relational views. This is a block editor, not
  Notion's full surface area.

Attracting these users produces refunds and one-star reviews, which cost more
than the installs are worth.
